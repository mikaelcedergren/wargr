# Private writing trials

27 October 2026. These are model-assisted editorial diagnostics, not a human reader study and
not an estimate of the probability that readers will consider future prose human.

## Material and method

Three fictional briefs were used for a paired comparison: an unsuccessful washing-machine repair,
a father's physiotherapy appointments, and a hypothetical magazine's cancellation policy. The
briefs supplied the same facts, intended meaning and length guidance to each condition. The
ordinary and warmer subjects tested range; they do not replace the author's preference for serious
subjects and emotional impact.

One fresh writer agent used the previous contract; another used the first candidate. Both generated
body prose only. A separate reader agent saw the briefs and A/B passages without seeing either
contract or being told which was new. The same underlying model family was used. Ordering was not
fully counterbalanced, repetitions were limited, and this did not exercise the deployed provider
or the complete JSON-output instruction. These limits prevent causal or reliability claims.

Private source material, candidate snapshots, outputs and the review are in ignored
`.run/verification/voice-lab/`. The fixtures contain no real biography or database records.

## Observed findings

The reader preferred the previous contract's repair piece because the narrator's wish to claim the
saving survived his recognition of his vanity. The candidate was preferred for the other two pieces,
mainly because their reasoning and events developed more clearly. This is a small diagnostic result,
not a success rate.

Both conditions still explained details after those details had already communicated their meaning.
The physiotherapy pieces repeatedly described what could not be inferred about the father instead
of allowing the observed improvement to register. The hypothetical argument also accumulated source
limitations in the prose. No substantial fabricated event or dialogue was identified by the model
reader; this is a limited review finding, not an independent factual certification.

The first candidate therefore did not pass as an established voice. Clearer and less showy prose
was not enough. Its positive character needed more force, and its safeguards were encouraging an
explanatory, administratively careful manner.

## Revision and additional probes

The candidate was revised to give the narrator more particular interests, let humour develop from
an observation, stop repeating a motive after conduct already shows it, and avoid narrating the
limitations of source notes. The author's subsequent directions were incorporated explicitly:
the assistant is a full ghostwriter; serious subjects and emotional impact are central; the author
supplies ideas rather than literary drafts.

Three additional briefs tested the revised candidate:

- A brief encounter with a station violinist produced a short account that kept enjoyment and
  irritation together without inventing a life-changing lesson. At 157 words, it did not expand
  toward the optional length range merely to look complete.
- Notes about an unspecified film produced a 30-word bounded account of the narrator's impression,
  without inventing a film or turning suspicion into evidence of intent.
- A final line-edit request on an already effective 86-word passage returned it unchanged.

These outputs demonstrate specific choices in these trials. The short film output also exposes a
product limitation: a complete-document response cannot communicate a missing factual hinge as a
separate editorial question. A short bounded draft is preferable to fabrication, but cannot be
called a completed long-form treatment of an unsupported argument.

## The author's reading test and acceptance

The Babylon rewrite was first prepared as a private candidate, not an edit to the published record. Its first
draft was reviewed against the original meaning. The review found improved exactness but excessive
presenter transitions, textbook sequencing of studies, and an abstract ending. It also caught two
places where the arrangement of the argument could imply an unsupported autobiographical chronology.
The reading version resolves those findings: it removes the implied autobiographical sequence,
uses Revelation's specific merchants and human merchandise, develops one explicitly hypothetical
fundraiser, and gives the distressed-but-obedient participant more attention. The comparison with
Asch and the repeated presenter transitions were removed. The woman/city connection is stated.

A separate Custodian pass approved this version for the narrow gate of showing a coherent,
meaning-faithful draft to the author. It checked Revelation 17:18 and 18:11–19 and Milgram's original
reported figures and distress description. That review was not approval of the voice or an audience claim.

The author then read the complete version, called it “2000% better”, and explicitly asked for this
direction across all stories and for the documentation and examples to be saved. The exact accepted
edition is now preserved in [voice-reference.ts](../../server/src/voice-reference.ts); the runtime
generation spec includes it with the accepted [voice instructions](../../server/src/voice-contract.ts).
[WRITING.md](../../WRITING.md) owns the workflow, and [examples.md](examples.md) explains selected
choices without maintaining a second copy of the complete reference or contract.

This is direct evidence of the author's acceptance of one complete piece. It does not establish
population-level audience response or repeatability across other subjects. Adoption in source is
separate from service delivery; deployment must be verified through the normal release process.
The logo and photographic image style remain unchanged.

The author subsequently clarified an exactness boundary through Slaughterhouse: the character is
a villain in a superhero film, and the reader need not know which one. This is now an explicit
contract decision. The earlier sparse-film probe tests avoiding invented identification and
unsupported creator intent; its very short result is not a target for deliberately unnamed but
otherwise intelligible material. Names are not a general prerequisite for an effective story.

The next explicit correction concerned pacing. The author found too many consecutive short
statements even in the accepted direction and asked for naturally longer development wherever
related clauses can sustain it, retaining the break when joining would be forced. He supplied an
exact four-paragraph opening to demonstrate how wording, punctuation and paragraphing establish
pauses, then explained that this requires understanding the meaning and where the story is about
to lead. These are accepted author preferences, now part of the canonical contract; they are not
an inference from a model score. The complete Babylon reference remains intact as historical
evidence of the first acceptance, while the later pacing preference governs new work. The
four-paragraph example is stored once in `voice-reference.ts` and supplied with every polish
request. The explanatory edits in [examples.md](examples.md) illustrate the feedback and have not
been represented as another full draft the author approved.

## Corpus adoption

The author then requested the voice across published and unpublished stories. Thirty-one records
were rewritten and saved through authenticated Admin controls: five published stories and twenty-six
drafts. Separate editorial passes compared the complete originals and rewrites for source fidelity,
composition and the later pacing preferences. Read-only checks confirmed all eight document fields
match the reviewed candidates, the original versions remain in history, and record identities,
publication states, dates and image prompts were preserved.

Two records remain unchanged: the author explicitly held “What I learned (rapid-fire session)” for
review because factual corrections would alter its stated health positions, and an empty preparation
draft has insufficient material to establish its intended subject. Neither is a completed rewrite.
Private originals, candidates, editorial concerns and the exact readback receipt remain in ignored
`.run/verification/voice-rollout/`; they are not training fixtures or additional approved exemplars.

These saves and the regenerated development preview do not establish a new public site release.
The full original Babylon reference remains the accepted edition, while the saved story also
incorporates the author's subsequent pacing preferences.

The author subsequently rejected the introductions under the titles. The earlier editorial review
had failed to catch that weakness, and its preferred correction initially confused an ingress with
a compact summary. The author's explicit correction is that it exists only to induce curiosity and
get the reader into the whole article, may leave its thought unsettled, and may end with “...”. That
purpose now governs the canonical voice contract, output instructions, Admin guidance and the new
ingress pass. It is a documented author decision, not a claim that the first rollout was successful
in every field.

## What remains unproven

Human audience connection, repeatability across multiple full-length serious pieces, the effect of
generating promotional fields in the same request, and production-provider behaviour have not been
established. Further evaluation should use the approved voice on fresh material and
retain concrete reader feedback. Passing format validation or receiving a favourable model review
cannot close those questions.
